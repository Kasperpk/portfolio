In the decade spanning from 2014 to 2024 FC Midtjylland won the Danish first division in football also known as "superligaen" four times and finished second in three seasons becoming the second most winning during that decade in the country. However they didn’t always dominate the league and as a fairly young club by football standards formed in year 1999 the story FC Midtjylland's caught the interest of many as we shall see. In 1999 two smaller teams located in the outskirts of Denmark was merged. By 2000 they promoted to the best league facing opponents like FC Copenhagen, Brøndby IF and Odense Boldklub which were if you know Danish football all teams with higher budgets and more professional experience at that time. While they in years following their promotion managed to stay in the best league they never really were a contender for any major trophies. Lacking both resources and attractiveness being located in the outskirts of the country it seemed that the club was doomed to be playing catch up and accept their fate as a middle tier team. They averaged 53,5 points in the seasons 2000/2001 to 2012/2013 having two second place positions as their best results in 2006/2007 and 2007/2008. In the following thirteen seasons from 2013/2014 to 2025/2026 they averaged around 64,5 points making them consistently playing for the top spots and four time champions. 

In 2014 FC Midjylland was close to going bankrupt and this is where Matthew Benham stepped in as a majority owner of the club. Benham was known for his enthusiasm with statistics and data through his ownership of the British football club Brentford FC. He had already made significant impact promoting them several times and culminating with their premier league debut in 2011. While bringing some financial strength to save the club from bankrupt Benham also arrived with a top priority to use data and analytics to create a competitive edge and support the core business. Several articles have been written about their success in retrospect. For example a title from IFREQ Football caught my eye "*The Use of Big Data by FC Midtjylland: A Revolution in Football Player Recruitment*" similarly Steve Douglas wrote in yourbasin the article  "*Data-driven approach taking Midtjylland to heady heights*". These articles describe how Benham changed everything from recruitment processes to the preparation for training and matches. For example instead of relying solely on observations by scouts and networks, they started using analytics for finding talent comparable to the popular story of the Oakland Athletics baseball team which later inspired the Moneyball movie. Benham had through his existing company *SmartOdds* access to data on players in many different leagues both within and outside Europe. Svend Graversen the Director of International Affairs & Academies described how the new recruitment process involved "*taking the data from Smartodds and combining it with our eyes (of scouts) who know which players can fit into Midtjylland*". Data was also used mid-game for tactical dispositions and to rank the team performance among rivals in the league but also against European competitors. 

The story of FC Midtjylland is one that displays analytical success and an inspiration for people like me that have built a career around succeeding with data. Across the Atlantic Ocean we find a similar striking story also within sports in the national basket association (NBA) in the United states. The San Francisco based franchise Golden state warriors participated in six finals in the decade from 2014 to 2024 winning four of them and being the only team to win consecutive titles in this span. Like FC Midtjylland their success has been analyzed by various experts where many tried to explain what made Golden State Warriors so dominant. A medium article from 2023 titled *Talent Was Not the Reason the Golden State Warriors Won, Data Analytics Was* attribute a lot of their success to their use of data. One of the things that they pioneered on was tracking every single shot players made both in practice and games. They used that data to guide players to make the best shots. "*The Golden State Warriors, for example, have effectively utilized data-driven insights to build their dominant dynasty. By understanding the significance of the three-point shot and exploiting mismatches, they revolutionized the game with their "small ball" lineup and fast-paced, high-scoring style"*. Inspired by the success of Golden State Warriors the NBA installed six cameras in every arena and as a consequence most teams in NBA now use data for these types of use-cases.      

The ability to collect, analyze, and *use* data seems to have been playing a key role in the success of both FC Midtjylland and the Golden State Warriors over several years. The world of sports have produced some of the best examples of what may be called the *analytical divide* where it has been quite easy to see how data and analytics can separate competition. I believe the reason for that is that sports offer randomized-control-trial like environment. What i mean by that is that the services that sports teams deliver are identical and clubs within the same league have typically a limited set of parameters which they can compete on; some obvious ones are financial resources, talent, training facilities and staff. They often go head to head competing for points which provides a very objective measure of quality and how they rank against each other. Teams with greater financial resources typically have an edge as it allows them to attracting better talent, build better facilities and hire the best staff. However there are also stories of teams that even against those odds have managed to compete and gain competitive advantages. 

However if we move away from the sports industry and look across industries there are several studies that find similar results. One such example is referenced in the introduction to *Data quality: empowering business with analytics and AI* by Prashanth Southekal. He cite a study from McKinsey (Böringer et al 2022) and Boston consulting (Manly et al 2022) which support the argument as they find that  *data-driven* organizations are found in top percentiles of companies when classifying the most innovative companies, but also when directly comparing raw economic data. Rick Sherman, the author of the Business Intelligence Guidebook refers in his book to study from Bain which showed that companies with advanced analytical capabilities outperformed competitors by having:

- 2x more likely to be in the top quartile of financial performance
- 5x more likely to make decisions faster
- 3x more likely to execute decisions as intended
- 2x more likely to use data frequently in decision-making

The effect of using data and analytics effectively is not therefore only accessible to organizations like FC Midtjylland and Golden State Warriors. It's found in industries like retail, transportation, construction and healthcare. Businesses like Amazon and Netflix are examples of companies that have integrated a lot of data into the core of their operations and with great results. While there is strong evidence that data and analytics can have measurable impact this book will go beneath that and look at why it may be that these success stories arise and why so many studies seem to come to the conclusion that organizations that master data also achieve superior outcomes. I devote an entire part of this book to this which i call *The value proposition of data*. We'll examine this question through the lens of decision theory, systems thinking, and the technological forces that have widened the gap between data-mature and data-immature organizations. This section takes a deeper look at mechanisms with which data, becomes information which becomes valuable. It's my perspective that this is a great foundation to have for any data professional. The second part *Running and managing businesses with data* asks the question that most managers and self proclaimed *data-driven* organization ask themselves everyday. Because how do we integrate data into the business and redeem the latent value lying in the digital objects that flow around in the wires all around us. The third part of the book takes a look into the machinery of producing those knowledge and data-centric products as i like to call them. We go deeper into *the journey to wisdom*, a process that have several analogies that i like to use to explain the concepts. Moving raw materials through a factory or move water through a water system can help paint the picture of what happens as the invisible electrical signals travel at the speed of light within and between hardware components. Finally the last part leaves with the message of getting practical experience and uses some success stories as inspiration to fuel this learning and work that is ahead of the student.
